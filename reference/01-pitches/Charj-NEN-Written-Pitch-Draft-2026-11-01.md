### What's your idea?

Charj is building a portable-charger rental network for **[1: first campus/neighborhood cluster]**. Find a station, scan a QR code, borrow a charger, and return it at another convenient Charj location. Our mission is to make portable power easy to borrow and return, so people can stay present in the places and communities that matter to them.

We are developing the service around community-shaped placement and return convenience. Borrowers can tell us where they actually use power and where they need a return point; those requests can guide host outreach and station placement. Voluntary stories can show how the service fits real lives, while a visible feedback loop shows which changes users helped create. This is our proposed approach within an existing category, to be tested against available local alternatives.

### What problem does it solve?

A low battery can interrupt a study session, an outing, or a working day. Finding an outlet may mean leaving the activity or staying beside a wall. A personal power bank helps when someone has brought it and kept it charged.

Borrowing introduces a second problem: returning. Someone may welcome power now and still hesitate if returning the charger becomes tomorrow's inconvenient errand. Upfront payment, charging speed, and the distance to an available return point all shape whether the service is useful.

We want borrowing and returning to fit the routes people already take. For hosts, the question is equally practical: does the charging service offer enough value to justify space, attention, and operating responsibilities? Charj must work for both sides.
