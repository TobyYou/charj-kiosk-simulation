TOBY

When was the last time you left home without your phone?

For our generation, that little screen connects us to our coursework, payments, maps, and family oceans away.

Then your battery turns red. You lower your brightness, check the percentage, and wonder if you have enough power to get home.

I own a power bank, but it collects dust. At night, my phone gets the charging cable. The power bank gets put aside. By morning, I want to take it with me—but I haven’t charged it.

Owning a backup battery doesn’t always mean having backup power when you need it.

And I’m Toby. Together, we founded Charj.

SOPHIA

Charj is a portable charger rental network. Scan a QR code at a kiosk, take a charged power bank with you, and return it at another Charj location when you’re done.

We plan to place our kiosks where people come and go: cafés, restaurants, bookstores, bars, gyms, and hotels. Customers will pay an hourly rental fee. Our local business partners will receive free installation and a share of rentals from their kiosk.

Connected locations make it easier to borrow near one stop and return near the next.

TOBY

We care because momentum matters to us: catching the next opportunity, capturing a moment, or sharing our lives with people far away. Sometimes, what interrupts that momentum is something as small as a dead phone.

With Charj, we want to give people the power to keep going.

SOPHIA

Seven months ago, we started with no coding experience, no hardware background, and no idea how to talk to a factory. But one question kept coming back: “How hard can it be?”

So we broke the work into steps. We built our software, worked with engineers, and visited two factories in Shenzhen. Along the way, I approached fifty-two local businesses. Some asked for immediate compensation. One florist gave me a rose.

TOBY

Then Ronnen Harary gave us a five-hundred-dollar grant and seven days to take our next step.

We bought twenty power banks, added our branding and a survey code, and lent them out for free. Through conversations and tabling, we gathered feedback from more than thirty students and faculty members.

One insight stood out: people often wanted a quick top-up to continue their day, rather than a full recharge. That reinforced our focus on fast charging and convenient returns.

Our little extra power traveled with us and our friends across New York, Tennessee, and Arizona: to lectures, parties, airports, golf courses, and backstage at a theater.

SOPHIA

Seeing Charj travel made us realize that a power bank is also something people carry and express themselves with.

During the challenge, we commissioned three local art students to design their own editions. They’ve started working. We want Charj to support local creativity and build a community that helps shape what we make.

TOBY

We believe now is the time because our phones are becoming our wallets, tickets, maps, and cameras. As more of our daily lives move with us, power needs to move with us too.

The response from our pilot gave us the confidence to take our biggest step yet. We borrowed from friends and family and made the final payment for our first fifty kiosks and four hundred chargers—before knowing whether we would receive the grant.

SOPHIA

With twenty-five thousand dollars, our first priority would be to repay the friends and family who helped fund production and shipping, estimated at about ninety-nine hundred dollars. We’d invest in a denser New York City network and plan to set aside around fifteen hundred dollars for artist editions.

Our goal is to grow from our first fifty stations to at least fifty more. We’ll learn from real use and returns as we decide where to expand.
