CharjCo’s Business Pitch to NEN & Ronnen Harary
Power Up. Take Action. Take Charj.
By Toby You and Sophia Peng, Co-founders of CharjCo LLC

Our Problem
As college students and members of Gen Z, we noticed the growing necessity of being powered up. Our livelihoods are becoming accessed on our phones: our university course work, dearest AI assistants, communication with our families oceans away, payment for the subway and dinner–even our car keys. Within the fast pace of our lives, our worst nightmare becomes a dead phone when we’re out. This anxiety is not unique to us: nine in ten people report panicking when their battery drops below 20%, a feeling now called “low battery anxiety” (LG, 2016). According to a 2025 Talker Research survey of 2,000 Americans, the average person starts to worry once their phone hits 38%, and Gen Z gets anxious even sooner, at about 44%. And what needs to be charged is not only our phones: our smartwatches, glasses, headphones–all of these devices we carry mobile throughout our day need to stay powered up. Keeping a charged portable on us at all times is heavy, tedious, and unaesthetic. Charj is our solution: scan to rent a power bank at a Charj kiosk–take it with you to keep your devices powered up wherever you are to capture memories, an important interview, your late-night-Uber back home, at the moments where you need it most. Return anywhere, so you can charge stress-free as you travel and take the day.

Business Model
Charj is set to build a portable charger rental network: encounter a station, scan a QR code, take a portable power bank, and return it at any convenient Charj location. Our kiosks will be placed in primarily private establishments where people come-and-go: cafes, restaurants, bookstores, etc. These will be done through partnerships with local businesses that will receive a percentage commission per rental from their kiosk location. Not only will partnered establishments receive free installation and monetary commission, they will also gain foot traffic from Charj users who walk in looking to scan for a power bank. Our revenue will primarily come from a per hour rental fee. A temporary hold will be put on a customer’s card after successful rental to promote timely returning on power banks; if a customer does not return within a certain timeframe–likely 24 or 48 hours–the deposit will be charged. This rental based power bank business model is tried and true in other parts of the world such as East and Southeast Asia, but is under-developed in North America. Charj hopes to adopt and deliver this model along with our own creative innovations, which we will detail below.

Why do we care?
“One of the most important elements of running a successful business is momentum… The key is to keep moving, keep products flowing, and not stagnate”
–Ronnen Harary, No Experience Necessary
We care about taking action. Momentum is essential to our daily lives. For us, it’s always about catching the next opportunity, documenting our present successes, and sharing it to our families oceans away. And, what kills our momentum oftentimes is something so minute: a dead phone. With Charj, we are not simply charging people’s devices; we are enabling them to work their flow uninterrupted–to keep going.

What have we done?
“There is an incredible amount of power in not knowing what you're doing, simply because you can't see the roadblocks ahead and so you don't know what's Impossible. When you have no self-imposed limits, you'll go further than you ever imagined.”
–Ronnen Harary, No Experience Necessary
We started CharjCo with no coding experience, no hardware background, nothing about the mechanics of a power bank, and no idea how to talk to a factory. But one phrase kept coming back up: “how hard can it be?” Chasing this question, we incorporated our company, parsed legal documents, certifications, built a full stack software including our web app, payments, database, and hardware connection to kiosks when just months ago Github was something completely alien to us. We also visited two electronics manufacturing factories in Shenzhen over the summer and are preparing a first production order of 50 kiosks and 400 chargers. When the steps were broken down, and when we self-taught ourselves across each stepping stone, without realizing, we had already crossed half the river–most efficiently, and without ever doubting whether something was possible, because it always was.

But this is all Day Zero. Here is where we progressed in the past seven days: first, we made a tangible prototype of our power banks and put them into people’s hands while our custom hardware awaited shipment. With our prototype, we were able to do two things: firstly, we verbalized our idea and our product to our friends and peers on our campuses through word-of-mouth and tabling. Through this verbalization, we were able to learn from 30+ students and faculty members who have used Charj in the past 3 days. One insight stood out: students often wanted a quick top-up to continue their day, rather than a full recharge. This reinforced our focus on fast charging and convenient returns. These early participants also became our first circle of testers - we set ourselves up with a community of peers interested in our idea and willing to give us insightful feedback. Secondly, we visualized the geographical expansion of where “our little extra power” could be taken. Charj accompanied us and our users around New York, Tennessee, and Arizona; within these places, it took us to lectures, halloween parties, the airport, the golf course, the backstage of a theater production, and the highrise office of a banking internship, to name a few. Seeing Charj travel with people made one thing clear: a power bank isn’t just something you use, it’s something you carry outwardly everywhere, and can become a part of how you present yourself.

Beside the foundational prototype of our chargers, we also wanted to explore how we can incorporate fashion into our product model, and help place mobile power banks into the culture of accessory in the new generation. Think Casetify: they don’t just sell phone cases, but also the unique and original artwork that comes with it, personalistic to your outfits, your mood, your favorite cartoons or characters–Casetify has become part of pop culture in this way. We seek to achieve a similar influence of culture in the future of Charj. We progressed this during our seven days by commissioning local art students to design one-of-a-kind editions of our power banks, developing our vision that our power banks can become trendy fashion accessories–something with personality, creativity, and aesthetic–accompanying you through your daily life and your favorite occasions.

Why Now?
“Surfing the zeitgeist doesn't always mean chasing the obviously trendy thing, like the internet in the late nineties or AI today. You can use technological and cultural shifts to re-invent and redefine any established industry.”
–Ronnen Harary, No Experience Necessary
As we depend more and more on our mobile devices in our day-to-day life, we also need a mobile way to keep us charged up. Surfing the zeitgeist for us does not mean becoming an AI company; rather, it means powering everything AI and our devices now ask of us. Our phones have become our wallets, tickets, maps, and cameras, and features like on-device AI drain them faster than ever. Mobile power demand will only continue to climb, and Charj seeks to meet that very demand. Furthermore, for the newer generations, what we carry is part of who we are: phone cases and charms have become part of fashion culture; power banks haven’t–yet. Charj seeks to invent our power banks into culture.

Our Next Steps (What would you do with $25,000?)
- The affirmations (validation) behind our actions in these 7 days gave us the confidence to take the final step in bringing our products to life and quantity - lending from our friends and family, we took the calculated and controlled–but nevertheless major–risk of confirming our products into production. Upon our grant, we will principally pay back our production and shipping fees. 
- Only when we have enough kiosk locations can the business model work. A secondary portion of our grant would be set aside for the expansion of installations concentrated in NYC. We seek to incentivize partnerships with local businesses with free installation and monetary compensation (percentage commission). We also seek to utilize our personal connections and help from friends and family who can help place us in their associated establishments. Our ultimate goal is to always have a Charj kiosk “as close as the next subway station” no matter where you are in the city. 
  - Demands: our door-to-door strategy asking 52 establishments around Morningside Heights, NYC landed us with 5 tentative yes’. In Boston, friends and family in the restaurant and cafe businesses have gotten us around 10 more tentative locations
  - Our first production order is really small: only 50 kiosks. This allows us to put our ideas into a tangible and workable product while controlling our input investment and under-surveyed demand. Going into production is also important to get our first partnerships too–without a tangible product, it’s a lot harder to get a solid handshake.
- Another principle goal with our expanded funding would be to invent our powerbanks into fashion and culture. We want to collaborate with and commission local art students in displaying their art to be printed on our chargers to be brought all around the world. Charj is innovative in that it seeks to expand the culture of accessorizing our most important and mobile objects. Like our phone cases, our power banks can become trendy fashion accessories–something with personality, creativity, and aesthetic–accompanying you to your favorite parties and concerts out.
