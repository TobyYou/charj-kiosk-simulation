Hi, I’m Toby, a Vanderbilt sophomore working on Charj: portable charging built
around student life.
When your phone is almost dead, you need power without putting the rest of your day
on hold. Our goal is to make borrowing and returning a portable charger fit naturally
into that day.
The experience we’re building is simple: find a station, scan a QR code, take a
charger with you, and return it at a participating station with an available slot. As the
network grows, returning one should become more convenient.
We’ve started by putting power banks into students’ hands, mostly through friends in
our early pilot. They asked for faster charging and more battery capacity. That
feedback is shaping our design.
We also asked participants to return the chargers after two days. We’re using this test
to learn about return behavior before deciding on a deposit policy. We’re exploring
ways to reduce unnecessary billing pressure, but those features still need testing.
I prepared my first pitch in about twenty minutes and received $500 in support. That
gave me a push to take the next step. Since then, [insert the actions completed after
receiving the grant]. We used [actual amount] for [verified expenses], with receipts to
show how the money moved the project forward.
Our proposed business model is portable-charger rental through convenient
locations. We still need to validate pricing, operating costs, and venue terms. We
want to build that service with our community: listening to criticism, testing
improvements, and sharing what we learn.
With a $25,000 grant, we would focus on [costed priorities] and test [specific,
measurable pilot milestones]. The next goal is to show that people can borrow, use,
and return a charger conveniently - and that the service can operate sustainably.
