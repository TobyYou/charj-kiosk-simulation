## 1. Cold open — the problem (0:00)

[VISUAL: Gray frame. A battery icon drains to red, the only color on screen. 8%… 7%… Low Power Mode pops up.]

**TOBY (V.O.)**
Eight percent. You dim the screen. Turn on Low Power Mode. Check it again. And again.

For our generation, that phone is our wallet, our dorm key, our boarding pass, and our ride home. Gen Z starts to worry at 44%.

[VISUAL: Toby pulls a plain black power bank out of a drawer.]

**TOBY**
And yes — I own a power bank. It lives in this drawer. At night my phone gets the cable, and by morning this thing is empty. Owning backup power doesn't mean having it when you need it.

## 2. Who we are (0:35)

**SOPHIA:** I'm Sophia.
**TOBY:** I'm Toby.
**BOTH:** And we built Charj.

**SOPHIA**
Charj is a power bank rental network. Grab one wherever you are. Return it wherever you end up.

## 3. How it works — idea + business model (0:45)

[VISUAL: Phone POV. Scan the QR code on a café kiosk → a power bank ejects → built-in cables.]

**SOPHIA**
Find a Charj kiosk at a café, bar, gym, or hotel. Scan the QR code, and a fully charged power bank pops out with three built-in cables. No cord to remember.

Pay by the hour, and drop it off at any Charj station — not just the one you took it from. A temporary hold on your card makes sure it comes back.

[VISUAL: City map. Kiosk pins appear block by block; dotted lines from "rent here" to "return there."]

**TOBY**
For the businesses hosting a kiosk, it's free. We install it, they earn a cut of every rental, and everyone walking in for a charge is a new customer through their door.

And every new station makes the others more useful. One kiosk is convenient. A network is freedom.

## 4. Why now (1:30)

**TOBY**
Across East and Southeast Asia, renting a power bank is as normal as grabbing a coffee. In North America, it barely exists — you either sit next to an outlet or carry a battery you forgot to charge.

And we're asking more of our batteries than ever: on-device AI, watches, earbuds, smart glasses, digital car keys. Demand for mobile power only goes one way.

## 5. The power of not knowing — why we care (1:55)

[VISUAL: Late-night laptop, code on screen → Shenzhen factory floor → dinner table.]

**SOPHIA**
Seven months ago, we didn't know how to code, build hardware, or talk to a factory. We had one question: how hard can it be?

So we learned. We incorporated CharjCo, worked through certifications, and taught ourselves to build our own software — the app, payments, database, and the link to our kiosks.

**TOBY**
Then we walked into two factories in Shenzhen, barely knowing what to ask. Instead of turning us away, their engineers walked us through every problem — and before any contract was signed, the owner sat us down for dinner and told us how he started. Not knowing what we were doing is what made us brave enough to begin.

## 6. What we did with $500 (2:40)

[VISUAL: Unboxing 20 power banks. Sticking on Charj logos. Survey QR code.]

**SOPHIA**
Our custom hardware wasn't here yet. So with our $500, we bought twenty power banks and cables, put a Charj sticker and a survey code on every one, and lent them out for free.

[VISUAL: Fast montage — lecture hall, airport gate, golf course, Halloween party, backstage at a theater.]

**TOBY**
In seven days, they traveled through New York, Ithaca, Nashville, Phoenix, and California. Into lectures. Through airports. Onto golf courses. Backstage at a theater.

**SOPHIA**
More than 30 people used them, and one insight stood out: nobody wanted a full recharge. They wanted a quick top-up to keep their day going. That's exactly what a rental network is built for.

## 7. What makes us different (3:20)

[VISUAL: Art students' sketches → four artist-edition power banks side by side.]

**TOBY**
We noticed something else: people showed them off. We decorate our phones, laptops, even water bottles — but the thing keeping our phones alive is a black rectangle.

**SOPHIA**
So we commissioned three local art students to create four original designs. Think Casetify, but for power banks. Rare artist editions get mixed into every kiosk, so every scan comes with a little suspense: will I get one?

That turns a utility into a collectible, gives people a reason to come back and post about it, and gets young artists paid for their work.

## 8. What we'll do with $25,000 (3:55)

**TOBY**
And we've already bet on ourselves. We borrowed from friends and family and placed our first production order: 50 kiosks and 400 power banks.

[VISUAL: On-screen budget builds line by line.]

**SOPHIA**
With $25,000:
**[$9,900]** covers that production run, so it goes straight onto the street.
**[$13,600]** builds density in New York, starting in Morningside Heights, where we knocked on 52 doors and earned 5 tentative yeses, with about 10 more lined up in Boston. It pays for installation and launch payments for our first partners.
And **[$1,500]** funds six new artist designs and 100 collectible power banks.

**TOBY**
Our goal: a Charj kiosk as easy to find as the next subway stop.

## 9. Close (4:30)

[VISUAL: Montage of the twenty chargers in people's hands. Music rises.]

**SOPHIA:** We started with no experience.
**TOBY:** Now we have momentum.
**SOPHIA:** We'll keep you powered up.
**TOBY:** You keep taking action.
**BOTH:** Power up. Take action. Take Charj.
