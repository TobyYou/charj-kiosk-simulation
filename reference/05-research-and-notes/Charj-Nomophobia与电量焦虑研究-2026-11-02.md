# Charj: nomophobia, low-battery anxiety and mobile energy practices

Research checked November 2, 2026 (America/Chicago). A focused evidence note for the existing pitch, not a new research project. Primary publisher, author and university-repository sources were used. Findings, inference and Charj-specific unknowns are kept separate.

## 1. Yildirim & Correia (2015): the conceptual foundation

**Exploring the dimensions of nomophobia: Development and validation of a self-reported questionnaire.** Computers in Human Behavior, 49, 130-137. DOI: [10.1016/j.chb.2015.02.059](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2015.02.059).

Publisher abstract reviewed; full publisher text returned access restriction. Nine initial interviews informed a 20-item NMP-Q, validated with 301 undergraduates. Four dimensions concern communication, connectedness, information access and convenience. This helps frame the functions people fear losing. It is not a test of charger effectiveness or willingness to pay.

## 2. Zhang et al. (2022): the closest battery-specific evidence

**Investigating Low-Battery Anxiety of Mobile Users.** IEEE iThings/GreenCom/CPSCom/SmartData/Cybermatics, 272-279. [Author-hosted full text](https://huangqy7.github.io/Paper/Investigating_Low-Battery_Anxiety_of_Mobile_Users.pdf). DOI: 10.1109/iThings-GreenCom-CPSCom-SmartData-Cybermatics55523.2022.00022.

Methods and findings reviewed. A 2019 WeChat survey yielded 2,044 valid responses, predominantly from China. Reported anxiety rose sharply below roughly 30%; higher-anxiety groups reported more frequent charging/portable charging. These are self-reports, not randomized effects or observed rental demand. The 2020 Tang et al. arXiv paper is related work, not an independent corroborating sample.

## 3. Robinson & Arnould (2020): the cultural/infrastructure argument

**Portable technology and multi-domain energy practices.** Marketing Theory, 20(1), 3-22. DOI: [10.1177/1470593119870226](https://doi.org/10.1177/1470593119870226). [University record and accepted manuscript](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/22401/).

Accepted manuscript methods/results reviewed. Interviews with 22 London commuters, aged 23-57, describe charging access shaping planning and mobility. This supports exploring charging along existing routes. The small, relatively privileged commuter sample cannot establish Manhattan demand, an optimal station count or universal behavior. Online publication was 2019; the journal issue is 2020.

## 4. Tuco et al. (2023): prevalence context, with limits

**Prevalence of Nomophobia in University Students: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.** Healthcare Informatics Research, 29(1), 40-53. DOI: [10.4258/hir.2023.29.1.40](https://doi.org/10.4258/hir.2023.29.1.40). [Open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9932304/).

Review methods/results checked. Twenty-eight cross-sectional studies included 11,300 students; 23 contributed to the meta-analysis. Pooled estimates were 56% moderate and 17% severe NMP-Q symptoms. Heterogeneity exceeded 90% for these estimates. These are questionnaire categories in studied samples, not a diagnosis or percentages of all US students. Do not simply sum separately pooled categories into a national prevalence.

## How Charj can use this

- **Research-backed problem framing:** anxiety about losing phone access involves everyday functions; battery availability and charging access can shape reported behavior and routines. Nomophobia is broader than battery anxiety: a charged phone does not solve every cause of disconnection.
- **Founder interpretation, not a research quotation:** “A low battery can change more than your screen brightness. It can change what you do next.”
- **Design inference to test:** convenient charging and return points may reduce disruption. Reliable availability, device compatibility and understandable fees belong in the service, not just more battery capacity.
- **One useful existing-survey question:** “Think about the last time your phone battery was low while you were out. What, if anything, did you change about your plans?” This is a custom market-research question, not a validated NMP-Q substitute.
- **Charj-specific unknowns:** whether the service reduces perceived worry, whether people pay, repeat borrowing, returns and useful location density. None of these papers establishes that Charj treats a phobia, proves its revenue model, or makes around 100 Manhattan stations sufficient.
