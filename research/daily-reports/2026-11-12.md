# Charj sim research — 2026-11-12 (scheduled run)

## Summary
- **First variance decomposition of profit (Sobol, 30,720 simulation runs).** Of the top 8 Morris factors, stocking (packs_per_kiosk, total-order index 0.32), rebalancing cost (0.23), the conversion rate c (0.22) and host share (0.19) explain most of the profit variance; c works mostly through its interaction with stocking. Two of the four are *decisions*, not uncertainties, so the next decomposition will separate them. Rentals/station/day depend almost entirely on c (ST 0.995), confirming M7.
- **Rebalancing cost is now grounded in wages, not a guess:** 12 → **$7/stop (4–15)**, from NYC's $22.13/h delivery minimum and BLS May 2025 courier wages ($19.78–21.69/h NY, $20.58 Nashville) × an assumed 10–15 min per stop. This adds about $90/wk to modelled profit ($427 → $516). Citi Bike's ≈$90 per bike rebalanced is a truck-fleet ceiling and does not transfer. The 2025 subway weekend factors replace the 2024 ones and settle yesterday's OD-vs-hourly puzzle: it was the year, not exits.
- **The simulator's price response is too flat at today's $1/hr price.** It gives an elasticity of −0.04, against comparables of −0.3 to −1.0 (ChargeSPOT) and −1.3 to −1.9 (Capital Bikeshare survey). So the model's "raise to $2–2.50/hr" profit gain is an artefact of the assumed WTP. It is flagged, not re-tuned: c and wtp_mid must be re-calibrated together. No audited US/EU per-station rental rate exists; operator blogs publish only showcase sites.

## Methods learned (→ STATS_TOOLKIT)
- **M4 update:** Saltelli/Jansen Sobol estimator run; ΣS1 = 0.75, so ≈25% of profit variance is interaction (mostly c × stocking). Lesson: separate decision variables from uncertain inputs before decomposing variance.
- **Price-response check (P088):** the logistic's point elasticity is ε(P) = −s·P·(1 − p(P)). With slope 2 and mid $3 it is −0.04 at $1/hr and −0.48 at $2/hr. Matching ε of −0.3 to −0.8 at $1/hr would need wtp_mid ≈ $1.2–1.9.
