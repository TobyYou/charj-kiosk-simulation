# Charj sim research — 2026-11-08 (scheduled run)

## Summary
- **Real data replaced or bounded 16 parameters** (P012, P013 and P035–P049, plus a correction to P031). The research came from 4 agents, a verifier and the orchestrator's own re-queries. 12 of the 17 web claims checked were confirmed outright, 5 partly confirmed, and none were wrong. All API numbers were re-run by the orchestrator.
- **Fitted from MTA data:**
  - Weekday footfall at 116 St is overdispersed: day CV is 0.08 and NB k is about 54. This is second-order at rental scale.
  - Post-event egress from Yankee Stadium puts 62–65% of the excess riders in one hour and about 90% within 2 hours.
  - The 8,000-person Columbia event scenario is a stress test, not a typical week.
- **The biggest unknown is still the share of phones below 20% in the evening.** No open dataset has it. Holding p_aware fixed, p_low_peak at 0.04 / 0.08 / 0.12 gives $142 / $336 / $543 profit per week (1.05 / 2.0 / 2.9 rentals per station per day, all inside the benchmark band).

## Methods learned (→ STATS_TOOLKIT)
- **M1, NHPP with a Gamma day effect (fitted):** footfall overdispersion is second-order for rental counts.
- **M2, event egress shape (fitted).**
- **M3, replication count:** 50 reps give ±5.5% on profit; ±2% needs about 380 reps.
- **M4, global sensitivity:** Morris screening, then Sobol (planned; the multiplicative funnel makes one-at-a-time sensitivity misleading).
- **M5, Gamma–Poisson with empirical-Bayes pooling** for pilot calibration.
- **M6, Raviv–Kolka station dissatisfaction function** as an analytic check on packs per kiosk.

## Couldn't find / dead ends
- **Hourly share of phones below 20% SoC:** not in Carat, Device Analyzer, LiveLab or Sherlock. The Hosio 2016 and Li 2018 PDFs could not be extracted, so their snippet figures remain unverified.
- **Event and battery measurements:** no modern 5G subway drain measurement, and no methodical survey of low-battery share at events.
- **Rentals:** no shared power-bank rental-duration distribution or non-return rate from any operator, and no evidence on how daily caps affect adoption.
- **Transit and footfall:** no hourly open data for WeGo, CTA or Miami-Dade. The Lower Broadway pedestrian count is locked in an image-only PDF.
- **Reference figures not confirmed:** Vanderbilt's Fall 2025 headcount (registrar PDF returns 404) and Columbia's football home-game count.
- **Not downloaded:** Citi Bike trip files (large; queued). The census API needs a key, so the work-around was Census Reporter.

## Biggest remaining uncertainties (top 3, by impact on profit)
1. **p_low_peak** (share of phones below 20% in the evening): profit ranges $142–543 per week over the plausible range. Only a street survey resolves it.
2. **p_aware**, calibrated jointly with p_low_peak: only pilot rentals can separate the two.
3. **Pricing structure:** $1/hr with no cap is above every comparable's daily cap. The effect on willingness and forfeits is unmeasured, and so is WTP (wtp_mid_hourly is an ASSUMPTION).
